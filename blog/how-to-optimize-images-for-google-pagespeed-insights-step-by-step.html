Why Image Optimization Matters for PageSpeed Insights

Google PageSpeed Insights evaluates your website's performance across both mobile and desktop devices. One of the most common recommendations it flags is "Serve images in next-gen formats" or "Properly size images." These warnings appear because images are typically the largest assets on any web page, and they have the greatest impact on your Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) score — a critical Core Web Vital that Google uses as a ranking factor.

When images are not optimized, they:

  • Increase page load time significantly
  • Consume unnecessary bandwidth for mobile users
  • Lower your Core Web Vitals scores
  • Reduce your chances of ranking on the first page of Google
  • Lead to higher bounce rates from impatient visitors

The solution is straightforward: compress and resize your images before uploading them to your website.

Step-by-Step Guide to Optimizing Images for Google PageSpeed Insights

Step 1: Audit Your Current Images with PageSpeed Insights

Before making any changes, run your website through Google PageSpeed Insights. Look at the Opportunities section and pay close attention to any image-related suggestions. Take note of which images are flagged, how much potential savings are listed, and what format they are currently in. This audit gives you a clear starting point so you know exactly which images need attention.

Step 2: Choose the Right Image Format

Not all image formats are created equal. Choosing the right format is one of the most impactful decisions you can make for page performance.

  • JPEG: Best for photographs and complex images with many colors
  • PNG: Best for images that require transparency or have simple color palettes
  • WebP: Google's preferred format — offers superior compression with no visible quality loss
  • AVIF: The newest next-gen format with even better compression than WebP

Whenever possible, convert your images to WebP or AVIF format. Google PageSpeed Insights will reward you with higher scores when you use these next-generation formats.

Step 3: Resize Images to the Correct Dimensions

One of the biggest mistakes website owners make is uploading images that are far larger than they need to be. If your blog post displays images at 800 pixels wide, there is absolutely no reason to upload a 4000-pixel-wide photo. Always resize images to match the dimensions they will actually be displayed at on your website. This single step can reduce file size by 60–80% before any compression even takes place.

Step 5: Enable Lazy Loading for Images

Lazy loading tells the browser to only load images when they are about to enter the user's viewport, rather than loading every image on the page at once. You can enable this with a simple HTML attribute:

Add loading="lazy" to your image tags for any images that appear below the fold. This reduces initial page load time and improves your PageSpeed score without any visual impact on the user experience.

Step 6: Use Descriptive Alt Text and File Names

While this step does not directly affect file size, it does impact your overall SEO. Use descriptive, keyword-rich file names and alt text for every image. This helps Google understand what your images are about and improves your chances of appearing in image search results.

  • Test your page after every optimization to track your score improvements
