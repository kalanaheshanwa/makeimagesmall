2. Squoosh — Google's Powerful Browser-Based Tool

Developed by Google, Squoosh is an incredibly powerful image compression tool that runs entirely in your browser. It offers side-by-side comparison so you can visually compare your original image against the compressed version in real time. You can also experiment with different codecs like MozJPEG, WebP, and AVIF.

  • Best for: Developers and tech-savvy users who want granular control
  • Formats supported: JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, and more
  • Key advantage: Advanced compression settings with live preview

3. TinyPNG — The Beloved Panda-Powered Compressor

TinyPNG has been a community favorite for years, and for good reason. It uses smart lossy compression techniques to reduce the file size of your PNG and JPEG images dramatically. The free version allows you to compress up to 20 images per batch, with a maximum file size of 5MB each.

  • Best for: Designers and marketers compressing PNGs in bulk
  • Formats supported: PNG, JPEG, WebP
  • Key advantage: Excellent compression ratios with minimal quality loss

4. GIMP — The Open-Source Desktop Alternative

If you need more than just compression and want a full-featured image editor, GIMP (GNU Image Manipulation Program) is the closest free alternative to Photoshop available. It's a desktop application with a steep learning curve, but it gives you complete control over export settings, quality levels, and formats.

  • Best for: Users who need both editing and compression capabilities
  • Formats supported: Nearly all common formats
  • Key advantage: Full desktop editing suite at zero cost

5. Canva — Design and Compress in One Place

Canva's free plan lets you create stunning visuals and export them in optimized formats. While it's not a dedicated compression tool, it's excellent for marketers who design images specifically for social media, presentations, or websites and want reasonably sized outputs without extra steps.
