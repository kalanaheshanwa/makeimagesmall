What Is WebP and Why Does Quality Setting Matter?

WebP is an image format developed by Google that uses advanced compression algorithms to produce smaller file sizes compared to JPEG and PNG. It supports both lossy and lossless compression, transparency, and even animation.

The quality setting in WebP compression works on a scale from 0 to 100, where:

  • 0 represents the highest compression with the lowest visual quality
  • 100 represents the lowest compression with the best visual quality
  • The middle range is where the real magic happens for web optimization

Choosing the wrong quality setting means either bloated file sizes that slow down your website or pixelated, blurry images that drive visitors away. Finding the sweet spot is essential.

The Recommended WebP Quality Settings

Quality 75–85: The Sweet Spot for Most Web Images

For the vast majority of website images — including product photos, blog thumbnails, and hero banners — a quality setting between 75 and 85 is widely considered the industry standard. At this range, you'll achieve:

  • File size reductions of 25–50% compared to JPEG equivalents
  • Images that are virtually indistinguishable from their originals to the human eye
  • Fast loading times that satisfy Google's Core Web Vitals metrics
  • A balanced trade-off between performance and aesthetics

Google itself recommends a quality setting of 80 as a reliable default for most web use cases. This is a great starting point before fine-tuning based on your specific needs.

Quality 60–74: Aggressive Compression for Speed-Priority Sites

If your website is extremely performance-focused — such as a news site, mobile-first platform, or e-commerce store with hundreds of product images — you might consider dropping the quality to the 60–74 range. This delivers:

  • Significantly smaller file sizes, sometimes under 20KB for standard images
  • Acceptable quality for smaller thumbnails and background elements
  • Excellent scores on tools like Google PageSpeed Insights

However, at this level, some quality degradation may become noticeable on larger images or high-detail photographs. Always preview your images before publishing.

Quality 86–95: High Fidelity for Premium Visuals

For photographers, creative portfolios, or any site where image quality is a core part of the brand experience, bumping quality to 86–95 ensures stunning visuals while still benefiting from WebP's superior compression over traditional formats.

Quality 100: Lossless Mode

Setting quality to 100 essentially enables lossless compression. This is ideal for logos, icons, and graphics with sharp edges or text. File sizes will be larger, but every pixel will be perfectly preserved.

Lossy vs. Lossless WebP: Which Should You Choose?

Understanding the difference helps you make smarter decisions:

  • Lossy WebP — Best for photographs and complex images. Use quality settings 60–85 for optimal results.
  • Lossless WebP — Best for graphics, illustrations, and images requiring transparency without quality loss.
  • When in doubt, lossy at quality 80 is your safest all-purpose choice.

How to Test and Find Your Ideal Quality Setting

The best approach is to test multiple quality settings and compare results visually and by file size. Here's a simple workflow:

  • Export the same image at quality settings 70, 75, 80, and 85
  • Compare them side by side at 100% zoom
  • Check the file sizes after each export
  • Choose the lowest quality setting where no degradation is visible
