What Is WebP and Why Does It Matter?

WebP is a modern image format developed by Google that provides superior compression compared to traditional formats like JPEG and PNG. It supports both lossy and lossless compression, along with transparency (alpha channel) support — making it incredibly versatile for web use.

The numbers speak for themselves:

  • WebP lossy images are 25–34% smaller than comparable JPEG files
  • WebP lossless images are 26% smaller than PNG files
  • Smaller files mean faster page load times and better Core Web Vitals scores
  • Better scores translate directly into improved SEO rankings

With browser support now exceeding 95% globally, there's virtually no reason not to be using WebP for your web images in 2024.

Understanding WebP Quality Settings

The quality setting in WebP compression works on a scale from 0 to 100, where 0 is the lowest quality (smallest file size) and 100 is the highest quality (largest file size). Finding the sweet spot between visual fidelity and file size is the art and science of image optimization.

The Quality Scale Explained

  • Quality 0–30: Heavily compressed, noticeable artifacts. Only suitable for thumbnails or background textures where fine detail doesn't matter.
  • Quality 31–60: Moderate compression with some visible degradation. Acceptable for non-critical supporting images.
  • Quality 61–80: The golden zone for most web images. Excellent balance of quality and file size.
  • Quality 81–90: High quality with minimal artifacts. Ideal for product photography and portfolio images.
  • Quality 91–100: Near-lossless to lossless. Very large file sizes — generally not recommended for standard web use.

What Is the Best WebP Quality Setting?

For most websites and use cases, a WebP quality setting between 75 and 85 delivers the optimal balance. At quality 80, images typically look visually identical to their originals while achieving dramatically smaller file sizes. This is why most professional developers and performance engineers default to this range.

Recommended Settings by Use Case

  • Blog post images and editorial photos: Quality 75–80
  • E-commerce product images: Quality 80–85
  • Hero banners and full-screen backgrounds: Quality 80–85
  • Thumbnails and preview images: Quality 65–75
  • Icons and logos with transparency: Lossless WebP (quality 100)
  • Medical, print, or archival images: Quality 90+ or lossless

The key takeaway is that quality 80 is your universal starting point. From there, adjust up if fine detail is critical, or down if page speed is the absolute priority.

Lossy vs. Lossless WebP: Which Should You Choose?

This is one of the most common questions around WebP compression. The answer depends on your specific content:

  • Lossy WebP works by discarding image data that's less perceptible to the human eye. Use this for photographs and complex, colorful imagery. It produces the smallest files.
  • Lossless WebP preserves every pixel exactly. Use this for logos, illustrations, screenshots, and images with text overlay where precision matters more than file size.

For the vast majority of web images — think photos, banners, and featured images — lossy WebP at quality 75–85 is the clear winner.
