Image File Size Limits for Major Platforms: Complete Guide 2024

Every platform you use online has strict rules about image file sizes. Whether you're uploading a profile picture on Instagram, sharing a product photo on Amazon, or adding a banner to your WordPress site, exceeding these limits leads to rejected uploads, blurry thumbnails, or painfully slow load times. This complete 2024 guide breaks down the exact image size limits for every major platform — and shows you how to compress image online free to meet them instantly.

Why Image File Size Limits Matter

Platform file size limits aren't arbitrary. They exist to protect server resources, ensure fast page loading, and create a consistent experience for every user. When your image is too large, platforms either reject it entirely or automatically compress it — often producing ugly, pixelated results. Taking control of your own compression means you decide how your images look, not the platform's automated system.

Using a reliable tool like MakeImageSmall lets you reduce file sizes precisely before uploading, keeping your visuals crisp and platform-compliant every single time.

Social Media Platform Image Size Limits

Instagram

  • Profile picture: Maximum 30MB (displays at 110 x 110 px)
  • Feed photos: Maximum 8MB for standard posts
  • Stories: Maximum 30MB, recommended under 5MB for fast loading
  • Reels cover image: Under 8MB recommended

Instagram recompresses images aggressively. Always upload at the highest quality possible after reducing file size, so the platform's second pass of compression doesn't destroy your image quality.

Facebook

  • Profile picture: Maximum 100MB (renders at 170 x 170 px on desktop)
  • Cover photo: Under 100MB, recommended under 1MB for speed
  • Shared photos: Maximum 15MB per image
  • Ads: Maximum 30MB, but Facebook recommends staying under 1MB

Twitter / X

  • Profile picture: Maximum 2MB
  • Header image: Maximum 5MB
  • Tweet images: Maximum 5MB for JPG and PNG, 15MB for GIF

Twitter's compression algorithm is particularly harsh on PNG files. Converting to JPG and using a free tool to compress image online free before uploading will dramatically improve the final appearance of your images in tweets.

LinkedIn

  • Profile picture: Maximum 8MB
  • Banner image: Maximum 8MB
  • Post images: Maximum 10MB per image
  • Company logo: Maximum 4MB

Pinterest

  • Pin images: Maximum 32MB
  • Profile picture: Maximum 10MB
  • Board cover: Maximum 10MB

E-Commerce Platform Image Size Limits

Amazon

  • Product images: Maximum 10MB per image
  • Minimum resolution: 1000 x 1000 px for zoom feature
  • Recommended file size: Under 1MB for fastest page loading

Amazon penalizes slow-loading product pages in search rankings. Keeping images under 500KB while maintaining high resolution is the sweet spot for seller performance.

Shopify

  • Product images: Maximum 20MB per image
  • Recommended maximum: Under 70KB for optimal store speed
  • Collection images: Maximum 20MB

Etsy

  • Listing photos: Maximum 1MB per image
  • Shop banner: Maximum 2MB
  • Profile picture: Maximum 10MB

Website and CMS Platform Limits

WordPress

  • Default upload limit: 2MB to 64MB depending on hosting
  • Recommended image size: Under 200KB for blog images
  • Featured images: Under 100KB for fastest Core Web Vitals scores

Squarespace and Wix

  • Squarespace: Maximum 20MB per image
  • Wix: Maximum 25MB per image
  • Best practice: Keep all web images under 500KB regardless of platform limits

Email Marketing Image Limits

  • Mailchimp: Maximum 1MB per image, 10MB total storage per upload
  • Klaviyo: Maximum 5MB per image
  • Campaign Monitor: Maximum 5MB per image

Email images should ideally stay under 200KB. Oversized email images trigger spam filters and cause campaigns to load slowly on mobile devices — killing your click-through rates before readers even see your message.
