Why Image Size Matters on Social Media

Every major social media platform has its own rules when it comes to image sizes and file limits. When you upload an oversized image, platforms automatically recompress it on their end — and they don't always do a great job. The result? Pixelated photos, washed-out colors, and blurry text overlays.

Here are a few reasons why optimizing your images before uploading is always the better choice:

  • Faster upload times — Smaller files upload in seconds, not minutes.
  • Better visual quality — You control the compression, not the platform.
  • Improved engagement — Crisp, clear images get more likes, shares, and clicks.
  • Reduced data usage — Especially important when posting on mobile networks.
  • Consistent branding — Your visuals look professional across every device.

Ideal Image Sizes for Major Social Media Platforms

Before you compress anything, it helps to know what you're aiming for. Here's a quick breakdown of recommended image dimensions and file sizes across popular platforms:

Instagram

  • Square posts: 1080 x 1080px
  • Portrait posts: 1080 x 1350px
  • Stories: 1080 x 1920px
  • Recommended file size: Under 1MB

Facebook

  • Shared images: 1200 x 630px
  • Cover photo: 820 x 312px
  • Recommended file size: Under 8MB (but smaller is always better)

Twitter / X

  • In-stream photos: 1600 x 900px
  • Recommended file size: Under 5MB for photos

LinkedIn

  • Shared image posts: 1200 x 627px
  • Recommended file size: Under 5MB

Pro Tips for Better Social Media Images

Compression is just one piece of the puzzle. Here are a few extra tips to make sure your social media visuals always look their best:

  • Always start with the highest resolution version of your image before compressing — you can always go smaller, but you can't recover lost quality.
  • Use JPG for photos and PNG for graphics or images with text — each format handles compression differently.
  • Avoid uploading images with large amounts of text — platforms often render them poorly.
  • Test your compressed image on mobile before publishing — most social media users are on smartphones.
  • Batch compress images when planning a content calendar to save time throughout the week.
