What Is HEIC and Why Are the Files So Large?

HEIC is Apple's default photo format, introduced with iOS 11. It uses advanced compression technology to store images at roughly half the file size of a traditional JPEG — while maintaining comparable visual quality. So why do HEIC files still feel large?

  • Higher resolution sensors: Modern iPhones shoot at 48MP or higher, producing massive raw data.
  • HDR and depth data: HEIC files can store multiple exposures and depth maps, adding to file size.
  • Live Photos: These include a short video clip alongside the still image, doubling the data.
  • ProRAW and ProRes formats: Pro iPhone models capture even more data per shot.

Understanding why your files are large helps you choose the right compression method for your needs.

Method 1: Change iPhone Camera Settings to Reduce File Size

The simplest long-term solution is to adjust your iPhone's camera settings before you shoot. This won't help with photos you've already taken, but it prevents oversized files going forward.

Switch to Most Compatible Format

Go to Settings → Camera → Formats and select Most Compatible. This switches your camera from HEIC to JPEG, which is more universally accepted and easier to compress later. Keep in mind that JPEG files may actually be slightly larger than HEIC at the same resolution.

Lower the Resolution

On newer iPhones, go to Settings → Camera → Photo Mode and choose a lower megapixel option. Shooting at 12MP instead of 48MP can reduce file sizes dramatically without a noticeable difference in everyday photos.

Method 2: Use the iPhone's Built-In Compression When Sharing

When you share a photo through the Messages app or Mail, your iPhone gives you the option to reduce the image size. In the Mail app, after attaching a photo, you'll see a size selector at the bottom of the compose window — choose Small or Medium to automatically compress before sending.

This is a quick fix for sharing purposes, but it doesn't actually reduce the file stored on your device.
