Why Google PageSpeed Insights Flags Images

Google PageSpeed Insights analyzes your webpage and scores it based on several performance metrics. Images frequently trigger warnings under categories like:

  • Properly size images – Serving images larger than displayed size
  • Efficiently encode images – Images not compressed enough
  • Serve images in next-gen formats – Using outdated formats like BMP or TIFF
  • Defer offscreen images – Loading images that aren't visible above the fold

Each of these issues can cost you precious points on your score and, more importantly, real seconds of loading time for your users. Addressing image optimization alone can sometimes improve your PageSpeed score by 20 to 40 points.

Step-by-Step Guide to Optimizing Images for PageSpeed

1. Choose the Right File Format

The format you use for your images matters enormously. Here's a quick breakdown of the most common formats:

  • JPEG/JPG – Best for photographs and complex images with lots of color. Supports lossy compression.
  • PNG – Best for images requiring transparency or sharp edges like logos and icons.
  • WebP – Google's preferred format. Offers 25–34% smaller file sizes compared to JPEG at the same quality.
  • AVIF – A newer format offering even better compression, though browser support is still growing.

Google PageSpeed Insights specifically recommends serving images in WebP or AVIF format to reduce file size without compromising quality. If your current tool doesn't support these formats, it's time to upgrade your workflow.

2. Compress Your Images Without Losing Quality

Compression is the single most impactful step you can take. Even reducing a 500KB image to 100KB can shave significant time off your page load speed. The key is finding the right balance between file size and visual quality.

There are two types of compression:

  • Lossy compression – Permanently removes some image data. Ideal for photos where small quality reductions are barely noticeable.
  • Lossless compression – Reduces file size without removing any data. Best for graphics, logos, and screenshots.

The easiest way to do this is to compress image online free using a dedicated tool. You don't need to install any software or have technical expertise. Simply upload, compress, and download your optimized image in seconds.

3. Resize Images to the Correct Dimensions

One of the most common mistakes web developers and content creators make is uploading images that are much larger than needed. For example, uploading a 4000×3000 pixel image when it will only ever display at 800×600 pixels is a massive waste of bandwidth.

Always resize your images to match the maximum display size on your website before uploading. This alone can reduce file sizes by 60–80% without any visible quality loss.

4. Enable Lazy Loading

Lazy loading tells the browser to only load images when they are about to enter the user's viewport. This improves your Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) score, one of Google's Core Web Vitals.

You can enable lazy loading simply by adding the loading="lazy" attribute to your image tags in HTML. Most modern CMS platforms like WordPress also offer plugins that automate this process.

5. Use Descriptive Alt Text and File Names

While alt text doesn't directly affect PageSpeed scores, it does impact your overall SEO performance. Google uses alt text to understand what an image depicts, which helps your content rank in both regular and image search results. Use concise, descriptive alt attributes for every image on your site.
