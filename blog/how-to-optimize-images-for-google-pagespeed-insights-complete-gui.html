Why Image Optimization Matters for PageSpeed

Google PageSpeed Insights measures your website's performance and provides a score from 0 to 100. Images typically account for 60–80% of a webpage's total file size, which means they have an enormous impact on your score. When Google audits your page, it looks for specific image-related issues including:

  • Oversized images that are larger than they need to be for display
  • Uncompressed images with unnecessary file data
  • Wrong file formats that aren't optimized for the web
  • Missing lazy loading for below-the-fold images
  • No explicit width and height attributes causing layout shifts

Addressing these issues can dramatically improve your Core Web Vitals scores, including Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) — two of Google's most critical ranking factors.

Step-by-Step Guide to Optimizing Images for PageSpeed

Step 1: Choose the Right Image Format

Selecting the correct file format before uploading is your first line of defense. Here's a quick breakdown of when to use each format:

  • JPEG/JPG — Best for photographs and complex images with many colors
  • PNG — Ideal for images that require transparency or sharp lines
  • WebP — Google's preferred format, offering superior compression with high quality
  • SVG — Perfect for logos, icons, and illustrations

Whenever possible, convert your images to WebP format. Google explicitly recommends WebP in its PageSpeed recommendations because it can reduce file sizes by up to 30% compared to JPEG at equivalent quality levels.

Step 3: Resize Images to Display Dimensions

One of the most common PageSpeed warnings is "Properly size images." This happens when you upload a 3000×2000 pixel image but only display it at 600×400 pixels. The browser still has to download the full-resolution image, wasting bandwidth and slowing your page.

Before uploading images to your website, always resize them to match the maximum size they'll appear on screen. For most blog content images, a width of 800–1200 pixels is more than sufficient.

Step 4: Enable Lazy Loading

Lazy loading tells the browser to only load images when they're about to enter the user's viewport. This significantly improves initial page load time. You can enable it simply by adding the loading="lazy" attribute to your image tags:

  • Add loading="lazy" to all images below the fold
  • Keep loading="eager" or leave it unset for your hero image or any above-the-fold images

Step 5: Set Explicit Width and Height Attributes

Always define the width and height attributes on your image elements. This allows the browser to reserve the correct amount of space before the image loads, preventing layout shifts that hurt your CLS score.

Step 6: Use a Content Delivery Network (CDN)

A CDN stores copies of your images on servers around the world, delivering them from the location closest to each visitor. This reduces latency and speeds up image delivery significantly — especially for global audiences.

Common Image Optimization Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping compression and uploading raw camera images directly to your site

  • Neglecting mobile optimization — mobile users deserve fast-loading images too
  • Forgetting alt text — it improves accessibility and helps with SEO
