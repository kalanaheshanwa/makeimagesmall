Why Image Optimization Matters for PageSpeed Insights

Google PageSpeed Insights measures your website's performance on both mobile and desktop devices, assigning a score between 0 and 100. A score above 90 is considered excellent. Images play a massive role in several key metrics that Google evaluates, including:

  • Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) – measures how long it takes for the largest visible element (often an image) to load
  • Total Blocking Time (TBT) – heavy images can delay browser rendering
  • Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) – improperly sized images cause unexpected layout shifts
  • First Contentful Paint (FCP) – large image files delay the initial paint of your page

When you optimize your images properly, you directly improve these Core Web Vitals scores, which also influence your Google search rankings. Image optimization is therefore both a performance win and an SEO win.

What File Size Should You Target?

As a general rule of thumb:

  • Hero images and banners: Keep under 200KB
  • Blog post images: Keep under 100KB
  • Thumbnails and icons: Keep under 30KB
  • Product images: Keep under 150KB

Step 2: Choose the Right Image Format

The format you choose for your images has a significant impact on file size and browser performance. Here's a quick breakdown of the most common formats:

  • JPEG/JPG – Best for photographs and complex images with many colors. Supports lossy compression for smaller files.
  • PNG – Best for images requiring transparency. Larger file sizes than JPEG but supports lossless compression.
  • WebP – Google's modern format that delivers superior compression compared to both JPEG and PNG. Highly recommended for web use.
  • SVG – Ideal for logos, icons, and simple graphics. Scales perfectly at any size.

Google PageSpeed Insights specifically recommends serving images in next-gen formats like WebP. When you compress image online free through the right tool, you should have the option to convert your images to WebP for maximum performance gains.

Step 3: Resize Images to the Correct Dimensions

One of the most overlooked image optimization mistakes is uploading images that are far larger than they need to be. For example, uploading a 4000×3000 pixel image when it will only ever display at 800×600 pixels on your website is a massive waste of bandwidth.

Always resize your images to the exact dimensions they will be displayed at before uploading. This simple step can reduce file sizes dramatically without any visible quality loss. Most online compression tools allow you to resize and compress in a single step.

Step 4: Enable Lazy Loading

Lazy loading is a technique that defers the loading of off-screen images until a user scrolls down to them. This dramatically improves initial page load times because the browser only downloads the images that are immediately visible.

You can implement lazy loading by adding a simple attribute to your image HTML tags:

loading="lazy" — Add this attribute to any img tag that appears below the fold on your page. Modern browsers natively support this feature with no JavaScript required.

Step 5: Add Proper Alt Text and Dimensions

While alt text doesn't directly affect file size, it plays a crucial role in SEO and accessibility. Google reads alt text to understand what your image represents. Additionally, always specify width and height attributes on your image tags to prevent Cumulative Layout Shift, which directly impacts your PageSpeed score.
