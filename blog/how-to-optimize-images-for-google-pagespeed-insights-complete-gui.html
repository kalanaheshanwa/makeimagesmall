If your website is scoring poorly on Google PageSpeed Insights, there is a strong chance that unoptimized images are the main culprit. Images are often the heaviest assets on any webpage, and when they are not properly compressed or formatted, they can dramatically slow down your load times. In this complete guide, we will walk you through everything you need to know about image optimization so you can boost your PageSpeed score and deliver a faster experience to your users.

Why Image Optimization Matters for PageSpeed Insights

Google PageSpeed Insights evaluates your website across several performance metrics, including Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), First Input Delay (FID), and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS). Images directly impact LCP, which measures how quickly the largest visible element on your page loads. A poor LCP score signals to both Google and your visitors that your site is slow.

Beyond user experience, page speed is also a confirmed Google ranking factor. This means that optimizing your images is not just a technical improvement — it is a genuine SEO strategy that can help your website rank higher in search results.

Common Image Problems Flagged by PageSpeed Insights

When you run a PageSpeed audit, you will likely see one or more of the following recommendations:

  • Serve images in next-gen formats — such as WebP or AVIF instead of PNG or JPEG
  • Properly size images — avoid serving images larger than what is displayed
  • Efficiently encode images — reduce file size without sacrificing visible quality
  • Defer offscreen images — use lazy loading for images below the fold
  • Remove unused images — eliminate images that are not contributing to the page

The good news is that most of these issues can be addressed quickly with the right workflow and tools — and you do not need to be a developer to do it.

Step-by-Step Guide to Optimizing Images for PageSpeed

Step 1: Compress Your Images Before Uploading

The single most impactful thing you can do is reduce the file size of your images before they ever touch your server. Many website owners upload raw images straight from their camera or design software, which can be several megabytes in size. A properly compressed image for the web should typically be well under 200KB.

You do not need expensive software to achieve this. You can easily compress image online free using tools designed specifically for this purpose. Step 2: Choose the Right Image Format

Format selection plays a major role in file size and compatibility:

  • WebP — the best choice for most web images; offers superior compression compared to JPEG and PNG
  • JPEG — ideal for photographs and complex images with gradients
  • PNG — best for images that require transparency, like logos
  • SVG — perfect for icons and simple illustrations; scales without quality loss

Google specifically recommends converting images to WebP format for modern browsers. Many online compression tools now support WebP conversion as part of their feature set, making this transition easier than ever.

Step 3: Resize Images to Display Dimensions

One of the most overlooked mistakes is serving an image that is much larger than it needs to be. For example, if an image is displayed at 600px wide on your website, there is no reason to upload a 3000px wide version. Resize your images to match the actual display size before uploading them. This alone can cut file sizes by 70% or more.

Step 4: Implement Lazy Loading

Lazy loading tells the browser to only load images when they are about to enter the viewport. This reduces the initial page load time significantly. In HTML, you can enable this with a simple attribute:

Add loading="lazy" to any image tag that appears below the fold of your page. Most modern browsers fully support this feature without any extra libraries.

Step 5: Use Descriptive Alt Text for SEO

While alt text does not directly affect load speed, it is a critical part of image SEO. Descriptive, keyword-rich alt text helps Google understand what your images depict, which can drive additional traffic through Google Image Search. Always write meaningful alt attributes rather than leaving them blank or stuffed with keywords.

Final Checklist Before Publishing Your Images
