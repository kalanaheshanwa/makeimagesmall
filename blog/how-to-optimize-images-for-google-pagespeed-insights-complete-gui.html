How to Optimize Images for Google PageSpeed Insights: A Complete Guide

If you've ever run your website through Google PageSpeed Insights and received a low score, chances are that unoptimized images are a major culprit. Images often account for the largest portion of a webpage's total file size, and when they're not properly optimized, they drag down your loading speed, hurt your user experience, and ultimately damage your search engine rankings.

In this complete guide, you'll learn exactly how to optimize images for Google PageSpeed Insights, why it matters, and how you can compress image online free using the right tools to achieve faster load times without sacrificing visual quality.

Why Google PageSpeed Insights Cares About Images

Google PageSpeed Insights evaluates your website across several performance metrics, including Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), Total Blocking Time (TBT), and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS). Images directly impact these scores in the following ways:

  • Large image file sizes slow down page loading, hurting your LCP score.
  • Images without defined dimensions cause layout shifts, lowering your CLS score.
  • Uncompressed images increase bandwidth usage and delay rendering for users on slower connections.
  • Improperly formatted images waste loading time compared to modern formats like WebP.

Google's algorithm increasingly rewards fast-loading pages, especially with its Core Web Vitals now being official ranking signals. Optimizing your images is no longer optional — it's essential.

The single most impactful thing you can do is reduce the file size of your images before they ever hit your server. Many people upload raw photos directly from their camera or phone, which can be several megabytes in size. PageSpeed Insights will immediately flag these as problematic.

2. Choose the Right Image Format

Not all image formats are created equal. Here's a quick breakdown of the most common formats and when to use each:

  • JPEG/JPG: Best for photographs and images with lots of colors. Supports lossy compression.
  • PNG: Best for images that require transparency. Larger file sizes than JPEG.
  • WebP: Google's preferred format. Offers superior compression with excellent quality for both photos and graphics.
  • SVG: Ideal for logos, icons, and simple illustrations. Infinitely scalable with tiny file sizes.

Whenever possible, convert your images to WebP format. Google PageSpeed Insights actively recommends this, and it can reduce image sizes by 25–35% compared to JPEG at equivalent quality levels.

3. Resize Images to the Correct Display Dimensions

One of the most common mistakes website owners make is uploading a 4000×3000 pixel image and then displaying it at 800×600 pixels using CSS. The browser still has to download the full-resolution image, wasting valuable load time.

Always resize your images to the exact dimensions they'll be displayed at on your website. Use your image optimization tool to set precise width and height values before uploading. This single step can save hundreds of kilobytes per image.

4. Enable Lazy Loading for Images

Lazy loading tells the browser to only load images when they're about to enter the user's viewport. Instead of loading every image on a page simultaneously, the browser prioritizes visible content first.

You can implement lazy loading with a simple HTML attribute:

  • Add loading="lazy" to your img tags for images below the fold.
  • Always keep loading="eager" (the default) for your above-the-fold hero images to avoid hurting your LCP score.

5. Always Define Image Width and Height Attributes

Google PageSpeed Insights will flag images that don't have explicit width and height attributes defined in the HTML. Without these values, the browser can't reserve space for the image while it loads, causing layout shifts that hurt your CLS score.

6. Use a CDN for Image Delivery

A Content Delivery Network (CDN) stores copies of your images on servers around the world, delivering them to users from the nearest geographic location. This dramatically reduces latency and improves load times for international visitors.
