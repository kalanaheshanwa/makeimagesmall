Best Image Format for Squarespace Websites (And How to Optimize Them)

If you've ever wondered why your Squarespace website feels sluggish despite looking beautiful, images are almost certainly the culprit. Choosing the wrong image format — or skipping optimization entirely — can dramatically slow down your site, hurt your search rankings, and frustrate visitors who don't want to wait for a page to load. In this guide, we'll break down the best image formats for Squarespace, explain how each one works, and show you exactly how to optimize your images before uploading them.

Why Image Format Matters on Squarespace

Squarespace is a visually driven platform. It's built for designers, photographers, shop owners, and creative professionals who want stunning layouts. But that visual richness comes at a cost — images can easily become the heaviest elements on any given page.

Google's Core Web Vitals now directly influence search rankings, and image load time is a major factor. A page that loads in under two seconds keeps visitors engaged. A page that takes five seconds loses more than half of them. Choosing the right image format and compressing your files properly is one of the highest-impact things you can do for your Squarespace site's performance.

The Main Image Formats Explained

JPEG (or JPG)

JPEG is the classic choice for photographs and complex images with lots of colors. It uses lossy compression, meaning some image data is discarded to reduce file size. For most lifestyle photos, portfolio images, and product shots, JPEG strikes an excellent balance between visual quality and file size.

  • Best for: Photos, backgrounds, hero images
  • Typical file size: Small to medium
  • Transparency support: No

PNG

PNG files support transparent backgrounds, which makes them ideal for logos, icons, and graphics with clean edges. The trade-off is that PNG files are typically larger than JPEGs. If you're uploading a logo with a transparent background to your Squarespace header, PNG is the right call — but make sure to compress it first.

  • Best for: Logos, icons, graphics with transparency
  • Typical file size: Medium to large
  • Transparency support: Yes

WebP

WebP is a modern format developed by Google that delivers significantly smaller file sizes than both JPEG and PNG, without a noticeable drop in quality. It supports both lossy and lossless compression, and it supports transparency. As of recent updates, Squarespace supports WebP images. If you can convert your images to WebP before uploading, you'll see real performance gains.

  • Best for: Almost everything — photos, graphics, logos
  • Typical file size: Very small
  • Transparency support: Yes

SVG

SVG is a vector format, meaning it scales infinitely without losing quality. It's perfect for simple logos and icons but isn't suitable for photographs. Squarespace allows SVG uploads in certain contexts, and since SVGs are code-based, they're often incredibly lightweight.

  • Best for: Simple logos, line icons, illustrations
  • Typical file size: Tiny
  • Transparency support: Yes

The Recommended Format for Most Squarespace Sites

For the majority of Squarespace users, WebP is the best overall format. It delivers smaller files than JPEG and PNG without sacrificing quality, and modern browsers support it universally. If you're running a photography portfolio or e-commerce store, switching to WebP alone can shave seconds off your load time.

That said, JPEG remains perfectly fine for photographs when WebP conversion isn't an option, and PNG is still the right choice when you need a transparent background and WebP isn't supported by your workflow.

How to Optimize Images Before Uploading to Squarespace

Step 1: Resize to the Right Dimensions

Squarespace doesn't need a 6000-pixel-wide image. Most Squarespace content areas are between 1200 and 2500 pixels wide. Before uploading, resize your images to match the maximum display size. Uploading oversized files wastes bandwidth and slows down your site even if Squarespace scales them down visually.

Before uploading, rename your files with descriptive, keyword-rich names. Instead of IMG_4892.jpg, use something like handmade-ceramic-mug-product-photo.jpg. Add meaningful alt text inside Squarespace's image settings. This improves accessibility and helps search engines understand your content.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Uploading raw files directly from a camera without resizing or compressing
  • Using PNG for every image, even photographs that don't need transparency
  • Ignoring alt text, which hurts both SEO and accessibility
  • Uploading multiple versions of the same image without cleaning up old media
  • Assuming Squarespace will automatically optimize your images enough on its own

A Simple Pre-Upload Checklist

  • Format: WebP for everything possible; JPEG for photos; PNG only when transparency is needed
  • Dimensions: No wider than 2500 pixels for full-width images
  • File size: Under 500KB for most images
  • File name: Descriptive and lowercase with hyphens
  • Alt text: Added inside Squarespace after uploading

Following this checklist every time you add images to your Squarespace site will keep your pages fast, your visitors happy, and your search rankings climbing in the right direction.
