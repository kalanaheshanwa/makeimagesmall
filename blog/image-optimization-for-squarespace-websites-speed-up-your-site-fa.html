Why Image Optimization Matters for Squarespace

Squarespace is a beautifully designed platform that makes it easy to build stunning websites. However, it has some known limitations when it comes to automatic image compression. While the platform does perform some behind-the-scenes optimization, it is not enough to compensate for uploading massive image files straight from your camera or design software.

  • Poor user experience — Visitors expect fast, seamless browsing. A sluggish site feels unprofessional and untrustworthy.
  • Lost revenue — Whether you run an online store, a portfolio, or a service business, a slow site directly hurts your bottom line.

Optimizing your images before uploading them to Squarespace is one of the highest-impact, lowest-effort improvements you can make to your website today.

How to Optimize Images for Squarespace: A Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1: Resize Your Images to the Right Dimensions

Many Squarespace users upload images straight from their phone or camera at full resolution. A single photo can easily be 5,000 pixels wide and several megabytes in size. Squarespace will display it much smaller, but it still loads the full file — wasting bandwidth and slowing everything down.

As a general rule, aim for these maximum dimensions before uploading:

  • Full-width banner images: 2500px wide
  • Blog post images: 1500px wide
  • Product images: 1000–1500px wide
  • Thumbnail images: 500–800px wide

Reducing dimensions alone can cut file sizes by 50–80% before you even apply compression.

Step 2: Choose the Right File Format

The format you choose has a significant impact on file size and quality. Here is a quick breakdown:

  • JPEG/JPG — Best for photographs and complex images with lots of color variation.
  • PNG — Best for logos, icons, and images that require a transparent background.
  • WebP — A modern format that offers superior compression. Squarespace supports WebP and it is worth using when possible.

Avoid using PNG for photographs — the file sizes are unnecessarily large. Switch to JPEG or WebP for dramatic size reductions without any visible quality loss.

Common Image Mistakes Squarespace Users Make

Even experienced Squarespace users fall into these traps. Make sure you are not making these mistakes on your own site:

  • Uploading uncompressed RAW or TIFF files — These formats are for editing, not for the web.
  • Using the same image at multiple sizes without resizing — Always resize for each use case.
  • Forgetting to add alt text — Alt text helps Google understand your images and improves accessibility.
  • Uploading dozens of large images to a single gallery — Squarespace galleries can become extremely heavy very quickly.
  • Ignoring your hero or banner images — These are often the largest files on your page and the first ones visitors encounter.
