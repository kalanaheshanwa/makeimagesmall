What Is WebP and Why Does It Matter?

WebP is a modern image format developed by Google, designed specifically to provide superior compression and quality compared to traditional formats like JPEG and PNG. The format supports both lossy and lossless compression, transparent backgrounds, and even animation — making it an incredibly versatile choice for web use.

Since Google introduced WebP over a decade ago, browser support has expanded significantly. Today, Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, and Opera all fully support WebP, meaning the vast majority of your website visitors can benefit from faster-loading WebP images without any compatibility issues.

WebP vs. JPEG vs. PNG: The Size Difference

  • WebP vs. JPEG: WebP files are typically 25–34% smaller than comparable JPEG images at the same quality level.
  • WebP vs. PNG: WebP achieves 26% smaller file sizes than PNG for lossless compression.
  • WebP vs. GIF: Animated WebP files can be significantly smaller than animated GIFs, reducing bandwidth consumption considerably.

These size reductions are not trivial. When you multiply the savings across an entire webpage with dozens of images, the cumulative effect on load time becomes substantial and measurable.

How WebP Images Directly Impact SEO

Search engines, particularly Google, place significant weight on Core Web Vitals when ranking websites. These metrics include Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), First Input Delay (FID), and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS). Images play a central role in LCP — the metric that measures how quickly the largest visible element loads on a page.

By converting your images to WebP format and choosing to compress image online free, you can:

  • Improve your LCP score by reducing the time it takes for hero images and featured visuals to render
  • Lower your overall page weight, enabling faster server response and delivery
  • Reduce bandwidth usage, which is particularly important for mobile users on slower connections
  • Achieve better scores on Google PageSpeed Insights and Lighthouse audits
  • Increase your chances of passing Core Web Vitals thresholds, which directly influence search rankings

Mobile SEO and Image Optimization

Google uses mobile-first indexing, meaning the mobile version of your site is the primary version used for ranking purposes. Mobile users are especially sensitive to slow load times — studies show that 53% of mobile users abandon pages that take longer than 3 seconds to load. WebP images help ensure your mobile experience is fast, smooth, and capable of retaining visitors long enough to convert.
