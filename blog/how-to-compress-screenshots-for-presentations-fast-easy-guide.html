Whether you're putting together a business pitch, a school project, or a client report, screenshots are one of the most common elements added to presentations. Why Screenshot File Sizes Matter in Presentations

Most people don't think twice about the size of a screenshot when they take it. But modern displays capture images at very high resolutions, meaning a single screenshot can easily be 2MB to 5MB or more. Add five or ten of those to a presentation and suddenly your file is over 30MB.

Here's why that's a problem:

  • Slow loading times — Large presentations take longer to open and can lag during live delivery.
  • Email attachment limits — Most email providers cap attachments at 25MB, making large files impossible to send.
  • Cloud storage issues — Uploading and syncing to platforms like Google Drive or OneDrive takes much longer.
  • Poor performance on older devices — A bloated presentation can freeze or crash on lower-spec laptops during a critical moment.

Compressing your screenshots before adding them to your slides solves all of these problems at once.

How to Compress Screenshots Before Adding Them to Slides

Step 1: Take Your Screenshots as Usual

Start by capturing your screenshots normally. On Windows, use Snipping Tool or press Windows + Shift + S. On Mac, press Command + Shift + 4. Save your screenshots to a folder where you can easily find and manage them.

Step 3: Check the Quality vs. File Size Balance

One common concern people have about compression is losing image quality. With a good tool, this isn't something you need to worry about. Lossy compression reduces file size by removing imperceptible data, while lossless compression maintains every pixel perfectly. For presentations, a slight lossy compression is usually ideal — screens don't need print-level resolution, so you can easily reduce a 3MB screenshot to under 300KB without any noticeable difference on a projector or monitor.

Step 4: Insert the Compressed Screenshots Into Your Presentation

Once your screenshots are compressed, insert them into your presentation the same way you normally would. In PowerPoint, go to Insert > Pictures. In Google Slides, use Insert > Image. You'll immediately notice the overall file size of your presentation is significantly smaller.

Additional Tips for Keeping Presentations Lightweight

Resize Images to Match Slide Dimensions

There's no point embedding a 4K screenshot into a presentation that will only ever display at 1080p. Before or after compression, resize your screenshots so their pixel dimensions roughly match your slide resolution — typically 1920 x 1080 pixels. This removes unnecessary data that would never be seen by your audience anyway.

Use PNG for Diagrams, JPEG for Photos

Screenshots of interfaces and text usually compress better as PNG files, while photographic images benefit from JPEG compression. Knowing which format to use can make a significant difference in your final file size.

Compress in Bulk to Save Time

If you're working with multiple screenshots — which is common in technical tutorials or product demos — compress them all at once. You can compress image online free in batch mode with tools like MakeImageSmall, which handles multiple files simultaneously, saving you a huge amount of time compared to processing images one by one.

Avoid Over-Compressing Text-Heavy Screenshots

Screenshots that include fine text, code snippets, or small UI elements need careful handling. Too much compression can make text blurry and hard to read. Always preview your compressed image before finalizing it, especially if your audience will need to read the content on screen.
