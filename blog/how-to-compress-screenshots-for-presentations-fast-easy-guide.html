Why Screenshots Make Presentations So Heavy

Screenshots are convenience captures — you grab them quickly without thinking about file size. But here's the problem: most screenshots are saved as PNG files, which are uncompressed by default. A single screenshot of a browser window can easily weigh in at 2MB to 5MB. Multiply that by 20 slides and you're dealing with a presentation that nobody wants to download or open on a slow connection.

Large presentation files cause real problems:

  • Slow loading times in PowerPoint, Google Slides, or Keynote
  • Difficulty sharing via email due to attachment size limits
  • Lag and freezing during live presentations
  • Poor performance on older laptops or low-memory devices
  • Increased upload time to cloud platforms like Drive or OneDrive

Compressing your screenshots before inserting them into slides eliminates all of these headaches immediately.

What Is the Best Image Format for Presentation Screenshots?

PNG vs. JPEG vs. WebP

Choosing the right format is the first step toward a lighter, faster presentation.

  • PNG: Best for screenshots with text, icons, or sharp edges. Lossless compression preserves clarity, but files stay large.
  • JPEG: Ideal for screenshots with photographs or complex backgrounds. Lossy compression dramatically reduces file size with minimal visible quality loss.
  • WebP: The modern winner. Smaller than both PNG and JPEG at comparable quality, though not all older presentation software supports it natively.

For most presentation use cases, converting PNG screenshots to JPEG and compressing them to around 70–80% quality gives you the perfect balance between sharpness and file size.

Pro Tips to Make Your Presentations Even Lighter

Crop Before You Compress

Crop screenshots down to only the relevant portion before compressing. Smaller dimensions mean smaller file sizes — even before compression algorithms do their work. If you only need to show a specific button or menu, crop everything else out first.

Avoid Embedding High-Resolution Retina Screenshots

If you're on a Mac with a Retina display, your screenshots are captured at 2x resolution by default. A screenshot that appears to be 1400 pixels wide is actually 2800 pixels. Resize it to 1x before compressing to cut file size roughly in half.

Compress Images in Batches

Handling screenshots one at a time is tedious. Look for tools that support batch compression so you can process an entire folder of screenshots simultaneously, saving significant time when building large decks.
