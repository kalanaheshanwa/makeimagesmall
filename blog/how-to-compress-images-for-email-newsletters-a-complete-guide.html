Why Image Size Matters in Email Marketing

Most email clients have strict size limits. Gmail, for example, clips emails that exceed 102KB in total size. When your email gets clipped, subscribers miss your call-to-action, your unsubscribe link, or your most important content. Beyond clipping, large images cause:

  • Slower load times — especially on mobile devices with limited data
  • Higher spam scores — email spam filters flag heavy emails
  • Lower engagement rates — readers abandon slow-loading emails quickly
  • Increased storage costs — for your email service provider

What Are the Ideal Image Specifications for Email Newsletters?

File Size

As a general rule, every image in your email should be under 200KB, and ideally under 100KB for hero images. Your total email size including all images, HTML, and text should stay below 100KB to avoid Gmail clipping.

Image Dimensions

Email newsletters typically display at a maximum width of 600 pixels. There's no reason to embed a 2000-pixel-wide image in your template. Always resize your images to match the display dimensions before compressing.

File Format

  • JPEG — Best for photographs and complex images. Compresses well with minimal visible loss.
  • PNG — Best for logos and graphics with transparent backgrounds. Larger than JPEG but crisper.
  • WebP — Modern format with excellent compression, though not supported by all email clients yet.
  • GIF — Used for simple animations, but keep file sizes very small.

Step-by-Step: How to Compress Images for Email Newsletters

Step 1: Choose the Right Image

Start with the highest quality version of your image. It might seem counterintuitive, but compressing a high-resolution original always produces better results than compressing an already-degraded image. Use original photos or high-resolution design exports.

Step 2: Resize Before You Compress

Resizing and compressing are two different steps. First, scale your image down to the actual dimensions it will display in the email — typically 600px wide for full-width images and 300px for half-width images. Sending a 4000px image that displays at 600px is a massive waste of file size.

Step 4: Check the Quality Before Sending

After compressing, always preview the image at 100% zoom to confirm the quality is acceptable. A good compression tool finds the sweet spot between small file size and sharp visuals. Most images can be reduced by 60–80% in file size with little to no visible quality loss.

Step 5: Test Your Email Before Sending

Use an email preview tool like Litmus or your email platform's built-in preview feature to test how your newsletter looks across different clients and devices. Check load times, image rendering, and overall appearance on both desktop and mobile.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using raw camera photos — These are often 5–20MB and completely unsuitable for email without resizing and compression.
  • Over-compressing images — Pixelated or blurry images damage your brand's credibility. Always find the right balance.
  • Skipping alt text — Many email clients block images by default. Always write descriptive alt text for every image.
  • Embedding too many images — A newsletter with 10 large images will always struggle. Use images purposefully and sparingly.
  • Not testing on mobile — Over 60% of emails are opened on mobile. If your images look bad on small screens, you're losing readers.
