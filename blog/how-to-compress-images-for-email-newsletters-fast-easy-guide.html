Why Image Compression Matters for Email Newsletters

Before diving into the how-to, let's talk about the why. Most email clients and mobile devices have limits on how much data they load automatically. When your newsletter is packed with heavy images, here's what can go wrong:

  • Gmail clips long emails — Once your email exceeds 102KB, Gmail cuts it off and shows a "View entire message" link, reducing engagement dramatically.
  • Slow load times drive unsubscribes — If your images take more than 2–3 seconds to load, readers bounce before they even read your content.
  • Mobile data limits matter — Many subscribers check email on mobile networks where loading a 5MB newsletter feels like a punishment.
  • Deliverability can suffer — Some spam filters flag emails with excessively large attachments or embedded images.

The solution is simple: compress your images before you hit send. Smaller files load faster, display better across devices, and keep your readers happy.

What Image Size Should You Aim For in Email Newsletters?

File Size Targets

As a general rule, aim to keep each image under 100KB. Hero images at the top of your email can stretch to 150–200KB if absolutely necessary, but smaller is always better. Your total email weight, including all images combined, should ideally stay under 500KB.

Dimension Targets

Most email templates are designed to be 600 pixels wide. There's no need to embed a 4000px wide photo from your camera. Resize images to match the display width before compressing — this alone can reduce file size by 70–80%.

Best Formats for Email

  • JPEG/JPG — Best for photos and complex images. Excellent compression with minimal visible quality loss.
  • PNG — Best for logos, graphics, and images with transparent backgrounds. Larger than JPEG but sharper for text-heavy visuals.
  • WebP — Modern format with superior compression, though not all email clients support it yet.

Stick with JPEG for most newsletter photos and PNG for brand graphics and logos. You can easily compress image online free in either format using a dedicated compression tool.

Step 1: Gather Your Newsletter Images

Pull together every image you plan to include in your email — hero banners, product photos, team headshots, promotional graphics. Don't skip even the small ones. Multiple small uncompressed images add up fast.

Step 2: Resize Before You Compress

Open your image in any basic editor (even Preview on Mac or Photos on Windows) and resize it to a maximum width of 600 pixels. If you're using a 2-column layout, resize each image to around 290 pixels wide. This step dramatically reduces file size before compression even begins.

Pro Tips for Email Image Compression

  • Always compress before uploading — Don't rely on your email platform to do it for you. Most don't.
  • Use descriptive alt text — When images don't load, alt text ensures your message still comes through.
  • Batch compress multiple images to save time when sending large campaigns.
  • Keep a compressed image library so you're not repeating the process for assets you reuse.
