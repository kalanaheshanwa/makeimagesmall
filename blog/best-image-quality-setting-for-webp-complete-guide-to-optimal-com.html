What Is WebP and Why Does It Matter?

WebP is a modern image format developed by Google that provides superior compression for images on the web. Compared to JPEG and PNG, WebP files are typically 25–35% smaller in file size while maintaining comparable or even better visual quality. This makes it an essential format for anyone looking to improve website performance, boost SEO rankings, and enhance user experience.

Whether you run an e-commerce store, a photography portfolio, or a content-heavy blog, switching to WebP and using the right quality settings can dramatically reduce page load times — a critical factor for both search engine rankings and visitor retention.

Understanding WebP Quality Settings

WebP uses a quality scale that typically runs from 0 to 100, where 0 represents the most aggressive compression (lowest quality) and 100 represents near-lossless quality (largest file size). Finding the sweet spot requires understanding your specific use case.

Lossy vs. Lossless WebP Compression

Before diving into specific numbers, it's important to understand that WebP supports two compression modes:

  • Lossy compression: Reduces file size by permanently removing some image data. Best for photographs and complex images.
  • Lossless compression: Preserves all image data while still reducing file size. Best for logos, icons, and graphics with flat colors or transparency.

For most web use cases, lossy WebP compression is the preferred choice because it delivers the most significant file size reductions.

Best WebP Quality Settings for Different Use Cases

Not all images are created equal, and neither are their ideal compression settings. Here's a breakdown of recommended WebP quality levels depending on your content type:

General Web Images and Blog Content

For standard web images, a quality setting between 75 and 85 is widely considered the sweet spot. At this range, images look visually sharp to the human eye while achieving significant file size reductions. Most visitors won't notice any difference from the original.

E-Commerce Product Images

Product images demand higher clarity since customers rely on them to make purchasing decisions. A quality setting of 80 to 90 is recommended for product photography. This ensures fine details like textures, colors, and labels remain crisp and accurate.

Hero Images and Banners

Large hero images and full-width banners are often the biggest contributors to slow page loads. Using a WebP quality setting of 70 to 80 is usually sufficient for these images, especially since they're viewed at a distance and slight compression artifacts are rarely noticeable.

Thumbnails and Preview Images

Thumbnails are displayed at small sizes, which means you can push compression further without visible degradation. A quality setting between 60 and 75 works well for thumbnails, reducing load times significantly across image-heavy pages.

The Golden Rule: Quality 75–85 for Most Situations

If you're looking for a universal recommendation, quality 80 is often cited as the ideal WebP setting for general use. It consistently delivers an excellent balance of visual fidelity and file size reduction. Many professional developers and image optimization experts default to this setting for everyday web images.

The key is to always visually inspect your compressed images before publishing. Even the best settings can occasionally produce artifacts on specific types of images, so a quick review is always worth it.
