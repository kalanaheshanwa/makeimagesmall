If you own an iPhone, you've probably noticed that your photos can eat up storage space surprisingly fast. Apple uses the HEIC format (High Efficiency Image Container) by default on iPhones to help reduce file sizes without sacrificing too much quality. But even HEIC files can grow large, especially when you're shooting in high resolution or capturing lots of photos on vacation. The good news? There are several easy methods to reduce HEIC iPhone photo file size — and some of them won't cost you a single penny.

What Is HEIC and Why Does It Matter?

HEIC is Apple's preferred image format, introduced with iOS 11. It's designed to store high-quality images at roughly half the file size of traditional JPEG files. Despite this advantage, HEIC photos can still be several megabytes each, and when you're dealing with hundreds or thousands of photos, those sizes add up quickly.

Additionally, HEIC files can cause compatibility issues. Many websites, apps, and non-Apple devices don't fully support the format. That's why knowing how to compress and, in some cases, convert your HEIC photos is a skill worth having.

Method 1: Change iPhone Camera Settings to Capture Smaller Files

Before you even take a photo, you can adjust your iPhone camera settings to produce smaller files right from the start.

Lower the Camera Resolution

  • Open Settings on your iPhone
  • Tap Camera, then select Formats
  • Choose Most Compatible to switch from HEIC to JPEG format
  • Go back to Camera settings and tap Video Recording to lower resolution if needed

Switching to Most Compatible will save photos as JPEGs, which are universally supported and easier to manage across platforms. However, keep in mind that JPEG files are typically larger than HEIC files at the same quality level, so this is more about compatibility than compression.

Method 2: Use the Files App to Share and Convert HEIC Photos

When you share HEIC photos from your iPhone via AirDrop or email to a non-Apple device, iOS often automatically converts them to JPEG. You can take advantage of this by:

  • Selecting the HEIC photo in your Photos app
  • Tapping the Share button
  • Choosing Mail or another sharing method
  • Saving the converted file back to your device

This method is convenient but doesn't give you much control over the final file size or compression level. For more precise control, you'll want to use a dedicated tool.

You can create a custom shortcut that resizes photos to a specific dimension or converts HEIC to JPEG with reduced quality settings.
