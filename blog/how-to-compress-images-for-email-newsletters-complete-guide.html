Why Image Size Matters in Email Newsletters

Before diving into the how-to, it's important to understand why image compression is so critical for email marketing success.

  • Slow load times: Large images take longer to load, and most subscribers won't wait. Studies show that emails taking more than three seconds to load see significantly higher abandonment rates.
  • Spam filter triggers: Many spam filters flag emails with unusually large file sizes, meaning your beautifully designed newsletter might never reach the inbox.
  • Mobile performance: Over 60% of emails are opened on mobile devices where data connections can be slower and less reliable.
  • Gmail clipping: Gmail automatically clips emails exceeding 102KB, hiding your call-to-action behind a "View entire message" link — which most users never click.

Recommended Image Specifications for Email

File Size Guidelines

Following industry standards for image size will keep your newsletters running smoothly across all email clients and devices.

  • Total email size: Keep the entire email under 100KB whenever possible
  • Individual images: Aim for each image to be under 200KB, with hero images ideally under 100KB
  • Width: Set image width between 600–700 pixels, which is the standard for most email clients
  • Resolution: 72 DPI is sufficient for screen display — there's no need for print-quality resolution in emails

Choosing the Right File Format

The format you choose can make a significant difference in file size without sacrificing visual quality.

  • JPEG/JPG: Best for photographs and complex images with many colors. Offers excellent compression with minimal visible quality loss.
  • PNG: Ideal for logos, icons, and graphics with transparent backgrounds. Larger than JPEGs but necessary for certain design elements.
  • WebP: A modern format offering superior compression, though not supported by all email clients yet.
  • GIF: Used for simple animations, but keep these small as they can quickly inflate your email size.

Step-by-Step Guide to Compressing Images for Email

Step 1: Resize Before You Compress

Many people skip this crucial first step. If your image is 3000 pixels wide but your email template is only 600 pixels wide, you're sending unnecessary data. Always resize your image dimensions first, then apply compression. This alone can reduce file size by 60–80%.

Step 2: Choose Your Compression Level

Image compression comes in two types: lossy and lossless.

  • Lossy compression permanently removes some image data to achieve smaller file sizes. For email, a quality setting of 70–80% is usually the sweet spot between quality and size.
  • Lossless compression reduces file size without losing any image data, ideal for logos and graphics where clarity is essential.

Pro Tips for Better Email Image Performance

  • Always include ALT text — many email clients block images by default, so descriptive alt text ensures your message still gets through
  • Host images externally rather than embedding them, which keeps your email file size low
  • Limit the number of images — aim for a healthy text-to-image ratio of at least 60:40
  • Test on multiple clients before sending, using tools like Litmus or Email on Acid
